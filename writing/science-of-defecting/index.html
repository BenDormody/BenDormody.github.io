The Science of Defecting and Current U.S. Politics

Abstract

Two people facing a payoff table that rewards trust nearly always cooperate. Five people facing the same table often don't, even though nothing in the table has changed. This paper models that shift as an n-player stag hunt: each added player compounds every doubt about the others, so cooperation that is rational in pairs becomes irrational at five. It then applies the model to the 2026 U.S. midterms, through the $5,000 “Trump dividend,” the one-point Senate races, Dan Osborn's independent run in Nebraska, and the mid-decade redistricting fight. Written on the eve of the election, with poll figures current to November 1–2, 2026.
